# Welsh listening reference

Reference for the `listening-layout` skill when the pack is `cy`. Everything here is for decoding what was heard. Any Welsh that goes into content from this file is still a draft until reviewed (see `docs/REVIEW.md`).

## Mutation decoder: heard form to dictionary form

A listener hears the mutated form and looks for the dictionary form. Present the table in this direction: **what you heard, what to look up**.

### Soft mutation (the most common)

| Heard at the start | Try | Example heard | Look up |
|---|---|---|---|
| a vowel, `w`, `l` or `r` | add `g` | `wyntog`, `law`, `wlyb` | `gwyntog`, `glaw`, `gwlyb` |
| `g` | `c` | `gymylog` | `cymylog` |
| `b` | `p` | `boeth` | `poeth` |
| `d` | `t` | `Swyddfa Dywydd` | `tywydd` |
| `dd` | `d` | `ddydd Llun`, `de-ddwyrain` | `dydd`, `dwyrain` |
| `f` | `b` or `m` | `fwy heulog` | `mwy` |
| `l` | `ll` | `am lifogydd` | `llifogydd` |
| `r` | `rh` | `dau rybudd` | `rhybudd` |

### Nasal mutation (after `yn`, "in", and `fy`, "my")

| Heard | Try | Example heard | Look up |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ngh` | `c` | `yng Nghymru` | `Cymru` |
| `ng` | `g` | `yng ngogledd Cymru` | `gogledd` |
| `mh` | `p` | `ym Mhorthmadog` | `Porthmadog` |
| `m` | `b` | `ym Mannau Brycheiniog` | `Bannau` |
| `nh` | `t` | `yn Nhywyn` | `Tywyn` |
| `n` | `d` | `yn ne Cymru` | `de` |

`yn` itself changes to `yng` or `ym` before these.

### Aspirate mutation (after `a`, "and", and `â`, "with")

| Heard | Try | Example heard | Look up |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ch` | `c` | `a chymylau` | `cymylau` |
| `ph` | `p` | `a phoeth` | `poeth` |
| `th` | `t` | `mellt a tharanau` | `taranau` |

A word heard with an extra `h` before a vowel: try it without the `h`.

## Sentence frames heard in a forecast

Teach these as frames with slots. Gloss each one literally so the listener can split what they heard.

| Frame | Literal | Natural | Slot filled from |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Mae hi'n ___` | is she in-state ___ | it is ___ | sky, temperature, wind adjectives (soft mutation) |
| `Bydd hi'n ___` | will-be she in-state ___ | it will be ___ | as above |
| `Bydd ___` | will-be ___ | there will be ___ | nouns: rain, showers, fog |
| `Mae hi'n bwrw ___` | is she striking ___ | it is raining / snowing | `glaw`, `eira` |
| `___ yn y ___` | ___ in the ___ | ___ in the ___ | compass, landscape |
| `___ o'r ___` | ___ from the ___ | ___ from the ___ | compass (wind) |
| `___ dros nos` / `drwy'r dydd` | ___ over night / through the day | overnight / all day | time words |

## Candidate gaps for a weather broadcast

Common in spoken forecasts and missing from the content today. **All unreviewed:** propose them, never add them as reviewed.

- Openings and closings: `Noswaith dda` (good evening), `Dyna'r tywydd` (that's the weather).
- Connectives: `ar y cyfan` (on the whole), `wedyn` (then), `ond` (but), `hefyd` (also).
- Time: `yn ystod y dydd` (during the day), `erbyn` (by), `tan` (until), `yn nes ymlaen` (later on), `bore fory` (tomorrow morning), `nos yfory` (tomorrow night).
- Quantity: `ambell gawod` (the odd shower), `cyfnodau sych` (dry spells), `hyd at` (up to), `rhwng ... a ...` (between ... and ...).
- Change and movement: `clirio` (to clear), `lledu` (to spread), `symud` (to move), `brafio` (to brighten up), `tua'r dwyrain` (eastwards), `ar draws` (across).
- Pressure systems: `gwasgedd uchel` (high pressure), `gwasgedd isel` (low pressure).
- Colloquial forms heard on air: `fory` (`yfory`), `pnawn` (`prynhawn`), `heddi` (`heddiw`, south). Use `forms` with `varieties` where a form is regional.
