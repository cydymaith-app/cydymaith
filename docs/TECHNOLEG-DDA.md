# Technoleg Dda

*Good technology.* The founding principle of Cydymaith am iaith.

> **Technology should serve the person using it, and ask nothing in return.**
>
> *Dylai technoleg wasanaethu'r sawl sy'n ei defnyddio, heb ofyn dim yn ôl.*

> **Draft.** The wording is a first draft for discussion. The Welsh is unchecked and needs a fluent speaker.

## Why the name

Hywel Dda (died about 950) is the only Welsh ruler remembered as "the Good". By tradition he gathered the laws of Wales into one code, *Cyfraith Hywel*, and those laws are remembered for their fairness. They favoured making amends over revenge, and protected people who might otherwise have lost out.

*Technoleg Dda* takes the same idea: fair rules, written down where anyone can read them, and kept. *Da* becomes *dda* after a feminine noun, as it does in Hywel's own name.

## The promises

Each promise is a rule the app keeps, not an aim it works towards.

1. **Nothing to sell.** *Dim i'w werthu.*
   No adverts, payments, upgrades, hidden fees or sales pitch. Nothing is locked away.
2. **Nothing taken.** *Dim yn cael ei gymryd.*
   No sign-up, no accounts, no tracking and no cookies. Your choices stay on your device and are never sent anywhere.
3. **Nothing hidden.** *Dim yn gudd.*
   - The app calls no AI service or language model, and makes no request to a third party.
   - The code and the content are open, and every phrase shows where it came from and its licence.
   - Anything not yet checked is marked as a draft.
4. **No nagging.** *Dim swnian.*
   - No streaks, scores, badges or notifications.
   - The app never asks you to come back. It helps while you are here, then lets you go.
   - The interface is unobtrusive. It follows from promises 1 to 5: with nothing to sell, nothing taken and a phone in one hand, there is nothing to interrupt you with, and extras stay out of sight until you ask for them.
5. **For everyone.** *I bawb.*
   - Made for a phone held in one hand, and it works offline.
   - It is readable in every palette, including high-contrast and colour-blind-safe ones, and usable with a keyboard or a screen reader.
   - It works without scripts wherever it can.
6. **Small and open.** *Bach ac agored.*
   Plain files and no dependencies, so anyone can read it, copy it, change it and host it.
7. **People first.** *Pobl yn gyntaf.*
   - The app is a companion, not a substitute.
   - A language lives between people, and the best way to learn it is with them.
   - Help is there where you need it: beside the feature, a few seconds away, asked for and never pushed.

## The test for every change

Before anything is added, ask:

> **Does this serve the person using it, and ask nothing in return?**

A feature fails the test in any of these cases:
- it needs an account, a server or a third party;
- it collects anything about the person;
- it works by bringing them back;
- it makes the app harder to use on a phone;
- it interrupts the person (a pop-up, a prompt, a banner) or crowds the screen with controls;
- it adds something to learn without its help, close to where it will be met.

A feature that fails is redesigned until it passes, or left out.

## Kin

*Technoleg Dda* does not claim to have started anything. It follows ideas that others set out first:

- [small technology](https://small-tech.org/about/), from the Small Technology Foundation;
- calm technology, from Mark Weiser and John Seely Brown, and later Amber Case;
- privacy by design, from Ann Cavoukian;
- local-first software, from Ink & Switch.
