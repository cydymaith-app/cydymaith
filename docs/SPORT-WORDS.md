### Upload 1 (a social post under a rugby video: Wales against New Zealand, a lit-up stadium during the Haka; written text, not live captions)

The post is written, so its spelling is reliable (circumflexes and all). Raw text kept as phrases only.

**Phrases**

| Welsh | English | Suggested home |
|---|---|---|
| Am wefreiddiol! | How thrilling! / Wonderful! | How it's said, Opening and closing (an exclamation; *am* + soft mutation is the "what a" frame) |
| Faint o ddylanwad gafodd … ar ganlyniad y gêm? | How much influence did … have on the result of the game? | How it's said, How it stands (*faint o* is new; *dylanwad* is in `change`; *canlyniad* is the singular of *Canlyniadau*) |
| Y goleuo anhygoel | the incredible lighting | Rugby, The match (*goleuo* new; *anhygoel* new) |
| Y gêm yn erbyn Seland Newydd | the game against New Zealand | Results and tables, Fixtures (*yn erbyn* new here; the country sits inside the phrase) |
| Yn Stadiwm Principality | at the Principality Stadium | Rugby, The match, or Teams and competitions (the ground named inside a phrase, never a list) |
| B'nawn Sadwrn | on Saturday afternoon | Time and date (*b'nawn* is colloquial for *prynhawn*; *prynhawn* is in content) |
| Wrth i'r ymwelwyr berfformio eu Haka enwog | as the visitors performed their famous Haka | Rugby, The match (*ymwelwyr* new, the visitors; *perfformio* new; *eu Haka*, aspirate mutation after *eu*; *enwog* new; *wrth i'r* + verb-noun is "as the … did") |
| Llongyfarchiadau mawr i'r criw tu ôl i'r gwaith paratoi | big congratulations to the crew behind the preparation work | How it's said, Opening and closing (*llongyfarchiadau* new; *criw* new; *tu ôl i* new; *paratoi* new) |

**Form**
- *Haka*: the name of the Māori war dance. Capitalised, taken as it is.
- *Eu Haka*: the aspirate mutation after *eu* (their): *Haka* has no initial *c, p, t*, so nothing changes. Not a mutation example; do not use it as one.
- *Gafodd*: soft-mutated *cafodd* (had, got) after the question frame *Faint o ddylanwad*.

**Left out**
- The flag emoji and the poster's account name.

**Questions for a fluent speaker**
1. *Am wefreiddiol!*: is *Am* + a soft-mutated adjective a normal exclamation, and is *gwefreiddiol* ("thrilling") the word usually used?
2. *Wrth i'r ymwelwyr berfformio*: is this the natural way to say "as the visitors performed"?
3. *B'nawn*: is *b'nawn Sadwrn* usual in speech and in writing, or only on social media?
