### Upload 1 (a social post under a rugby video: Wales against New Zealand, a lit-up stadium during the Haka; written text, not live captions)

The post is written, so its spelling is reliable (circumflexes and all). Raw text kept as phrases only.

**Phrases**

| Welsh | English | Suggested home |
|---|---|---|
| Am wefreiddiol! | How thrilling! / Wonderful! | How it's said, Opening and closing (an exclamation; *am* + soft mutation is the "what a" frame) |
| Faint o ddylanwad gafodd … ar ganlyniad y gêm? | How much influence did … have on the result of the game? | How it's said, How it stands (*faint o* is new; *dylanwad* is in `change`; *canlyniad* is the singular of *Canlyniadau*) |
| Y goleuo anhygoel | the incredible lighting | Rugby, The match (*goleuo* new; *anhygoel* new) |
| Y gêm yn erbyn Seland Newydd | the game against New Zealand | Results and tables, Fixtures (*yn erbyn* new here; the country sits inside the phrase) |
| Yn Stadiwm Principality | at the Principality Stadium | Rugby, The match, or Teams and competitions (the ground named inside a phrase, never a list) |
| B'nawn Sadwrn | on Saturday afternoon | Time and date (*b'nawn* is colloquial for *prynhawn*; *prynhawn* is in content) |
| Wrth i'r ymwelwyr berfformio eu Haka enwog | as the visitors performed their famous Haka | Rugby, The match (*ymwelwyr* new, the visitors; *perfformio* new; *eu Haka*, aspirate mutation after *eu*; *enwog* new; *wrth i'r* + verb-noun is "as the … did") |
| Llongyfarchiadau mawr i'r criw tu ôl i'r gwaith paratoi | big congratulations to the crew behind the preparation work | How it's said, Opening and closing (*llongyfarchiadau* new; *criw* new; *tu ôl i* new; *paratoi* new) |

**Form**
- *Haka*: the name of the Māori war dance. Capitalised, taken as it is.
- *Eu Haka*: the aspirate mutation after *eu* (their): *Haka* has no initial *c, p, t*, so nothing changes. Not a mutation example; do not use it as one.
- *Gafodd*: soft-mutated *cafodd* (had, got) after the question frame *Faint o ddylanwad*.

**Left out**
- The flag emoji and the poster's account name.

**Questions for a fluent speaker**
1. *Am wefreiddiol!*: is *Am* + a soft-mutated adjective a normal exclamation, and is *gwefreiddiol* ("thrilling") the word usually used?
2. *Wrth i'r ymwelwyr berfformio*: is this the natural way to say "as the visitors performed"?
3. *B'nawn*: is *b'nawn Sadwrn* usual in speech and in writing, or only on social media?

### Upload 2 (a second social post, the same match: the reaction to the win; written text)

**Phrases**

| Welsh | English | Suggested home |
|---|---|---|
| Mae Cymru wedi curo Seland Newydd | Wales have beaten New Zealand | How it's said, Who scored / How it stands (*curo* new; pairs with *Mae Cymru wedi ennill*, which is in `sport-patterns`) |
| Pwy fase'n meddwl? | Who would have thought? | How it's said, Opening and closing (a reaction; *fase'n* is colloquial for *byddai'n*, the conditional, *pwy* + *meddwl*; *meddwl* new) |
| Yr ymateb | the reaction | How it's said (*ymateb* new) |
| Wedi buddugoliaeth hanesyddol Cymru | after Wales's historic victory | Results and tables, Scores (*buddugoliaeth* new; *hanesyddol* new; *wedi* + noun is "after") |
| Dros y Crysau Duon | over the All Blacks | Teams and competitions, Teams (*Crysau Duon*, literally "Black Shirts", is the Welsh name for the All Blacks; *dros* here is "over, against" a team) |
| O 35-28 | by 35 to 28 | Results and tables, Scores (compare *Ennill o ddeg pwynt*, which is in content; here *o* + the score) |
| Brynhawn Sadwrn | on Saturday afternoon | Time and date (the time phrase is soft-mutated, no *yn*; *prynhawn* is in content; upload 1 had *b'nawn*) |

**Form**
- *Brynhawn*: soft mutation of *prynhawn*, as a time phrase with no preposition.
- *Fase'n*: colloquial and southern, for *byddai'n*.
- *Crysau Duon*: the adjective follows a plural noun and is not changed (*duon*, the plural of *du*, agrees with *crysau*). *Crysau* is a plural of *crys*; both are in everyday clothing words if present, check.

**Left out**
- The emoji and the poster's account name.

**Questions for a fluent speaker**
1. *Mae Cymru wedi curo …* against *Mae Cymru wedi ennill …*: is *curo* the usual verb for beating a named team?
2. *Dros y Crysau Duon*: is *dros* the usual preposition for a win over a team, or *yn erbyn*, or *ar*?
3. *Pwy fase'n meddwl?*: is *fase'n* usual, or *fyddai'n* in a written post?
4. *Crysau Duon*: is it always capitalised as a name?

### Upload 3 (a third social post, the same match, linking to an S4C news article; written text)

**Phrases**

| Welsh | English | Suggested home |
|---|---|---|
| Buddugoliaeth o'r diwedd! | A win at last! | How it's said, Opening and closing (*o'r diwedd* new; *buddugoliaeth* held from upload 2) |
| Cymru yn curo Seland Newydd | Wales beating New Zealand | Results and tables, Scores (the present participle as a headline: *yn curo* with no *mae*; *curo* held from upload 2) |
| Am y tro cyntaf ers 73 o flynyddoedd | for the first time in 73 years | Results and tables, Scores (*am y tro cyntaf* new; *ers* new, since; *blynyddoedd* is the plural of *blwyddyn*; *73 o flynyddoedd*, *o* after a number) |
| Mewn gêm anhygoel | in an incredible game | Rugby, The match (*anhygoel* held from upload 1; *mewn gêm*, with *gêm* unmutated after *mewn*) |
| Bnawn Sadwrn | on Saturday afternoon | Time and date (colloquial *prynhawn*, here *bnawn*; upload 1 had *b'nawn*, upload 2 had *brynhawn*) |

**Form**
- *Ers 73 o flynyddoedd*: *ers* takes a period of time. *Blynyddoedd* is the plural used after a number with *o* (*73 o flynyddoedd*), where the singular *blwyddyn* would be used after a number in other styles.
- *Yn curo* as a headline: the present participle stands for the past ("Wales beat"), as English headlines use the present.

**Same-topic notes**
- The three posts give three forms of "Saturday afternoon" (*b'nawn*, *brynhawn*, *bnawn*). The first and third are colloquial; the second is the full soft-mutated form.
- The link is to an S4C news article (the address is not kept). The headline in its address reads *Cymru'n curo Seland Newydd am y tro cyntaf mewn 73 o flynyddoedd*, where the post says *ers*: both are heard for "in 73 years".

**Left out**
- The emoji, the poster's account name and the article address.

**Questions for a fluent speaker**
1. *Am y tro cyntaf ers 73 o flynyddoedd* against *am y tro cyntaf mewn 73 o flynyddoedd*: are both usual?
2. *Cymru yn curo* as a past-tense headline: is it normal in written Welsh, or only in news and social posts?
3. *Bnawn*: is this spelling used, or only *b'nawn*?

**Form**

**Typos and gaps in the post**
- *angen i Gymru well ar eu perfformiad*: the verb looks incomplete, perhaps *wella* with *ar* wrongly added. Read as *gwella* (to improve) for the draft, and check before using. Do not raise the slip again if it recurs; written posts carry typos too.

**Same-topic notes**
- This post is a preview of the same fixture: the match was on the Saturday, and the earlier uploads are the match and its result. The tense changes with it (*wrth i … ymweld*, *mae angen*).

**Left out**
- The emoji, the poster's account name and the player's name.

**Questions for a fluent speaker**
1. *Mae llawer o bethau ni'n gallu gwneud yn well*: is the missing *y* (*y gallwn ni wneud*, "that we can do") normally dropped like this in speech?
2. *Bod angen i Gymru wella eu perfformiad*: confirm the word is *wella*, and that *bod angen i* is the usual frame.

### Upload 5 (football, international: a result post; written text)

**Phrases**

| Welsh | English | Suggested home |
|---|---|---|
| Siom i Gymru | Disappointment for Wales | How it's said, Opening and closing, or Results and tables, Scores (*siom* new; *i* "for") |
| Ar ôl colli 3-1 | after losing 3-1 | Results and tables, Scores (*ar ôl* held as a phrase from the forecast words; *colli* new, to lose; the score is said as figures, check how it is read) |
| Gyda thîm Bellamy | with Bellamy's team | Teams and competitions, Teams (*gyda* with the aspirate mutation, *tîm* becoming *thîm*; *tîm* [person's name]: the manager's name stays out of the app) |
| Yn gorffen ar waelod y grŵp | finishing at the bottom of the group | Results and tables, The table (*gorffen* new; *gwaelod* new, with *waelod* soft-mutated after *ar*; *grŵp* new) |
| Gydag un gêm yn weddill | with one game remaining | Results and tables, Fixtures (*yn weddill* new, remaining; *gweddill* held from the forecast words) |
| O'r ymgyrch | of the campaign | Teams and competitions, Competitions (*ymgyrch* new, a campaign or qualifying run) |

**Form**
- *Thîm*: aspirate mutation of *tîm* after *gyda* (with). *Gydag* is used before a vowel, as in *gydag un gêm*; *gyda* before a consonant, as in *gyda thîm*.
- *Waelod*: soft mutation of *gwaelod* after *ar*.
- *Weddill* (here *yn weddill*): soft mutation of *gweddill* after *yn* (in a predicate, "remaining").
- *Colli 3-1*: the score follows the verb-noun directly, with no preposition.

**Pattern**
- A match report in one line: the feeling, then *ar ôl* with the result, then *gyda* and the consequence. This shape is worth a row on How it's said, Opening and closing.

**Left out**
- The emoji, the poster's account name and the manager's name (a person's name, not vocabulary).

**Questions for a fluent speaker**
1. *Siom i Gymru*: is *siom* the usual noun here, or *siomedigaeth*?
2. *Colli 3-1*: how is the score said aloud (*tri un*, *tri i un*)?
3. *Gwaelod y grŵp*: is *grŵp* the usual word for a qualifying group, and is *ymgyrch* the usual word for the qualifying campaign?
4. *Gydag un gêm yn weddill*: confirm the form of *yn weddill*.
